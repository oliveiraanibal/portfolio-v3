In short

The problem

A lighter, self-service tier was due in Q2, but its onboarding lived in seven documents that didn't agree, and the first set-up steps took about 8 hours of emails and calls per customer.

What I did

Helped turn the seven sources into one service blueprint, and led the design of the three set-up features a bank meets first: SSO, IP allowlist and API credentials.

The outcome

7 sources, 1 shared blueprint

Three self-service set-up features, designed to remove the manual back and forth at the start of every customer.

Context and my role

Feedzai builds fraud and anti-money-laundering software for banks. RiskOps Studio, rolling out since June 2025, is its unified platform, gradually replacing the separate Fraud, AML and Identity applications. In 2026 Feedzai was preparing TFB Growth, a lighter and more self-service entry tier of Transaction Fraud for Banking, due by the end of Q2. I joined on a contract as the main designer for onboarding and activation.

My part

  • Synthesis for the service blueprint: AI-assisted extraction from every source, checked with the owners.
  • Lead design of SSO, IP allowlist, API client credentials and the Getting Started page.
  • Specs with engineering in Kiro, a spec-driven AI tool, built from design-system components.

The team's part

  • User researchers and product managers led the blueprint and its decisions.
  • Engineering built the features from the specs.
  • Customer success and the service team brought what really happens with banks today.

The problemA journey nobody could see whole

Ten Feedzai teams and up to seven roles at the bank each owned a slice of onboarding. What was known sat in seven documents written by different teams, and they didn't always agree.

The very first step was also the most manual. Customer success, project managers and the service team collected identity-provider details, IP lists and API key requests by email and on calls: about 8 hours per customer, before the bank saw any value.

What I learnedExisting material is research data

We treated the documents as research data. I used AI to pull steps, actors, systems and open questions out of each source; we then checked every item against the original and with the team that owned it, and logged conflicts instead of picking a side. The result was a blueprint of 10 stages in 4 phases, from contract to support and expansion.

  • The first login was a hand-off, not a product moment.a Getting Started page lists the three set-up steps and the status of each.
  • Each environment needs its own setup, and the wrong one is easy to configure.every set-up page names its environment first.
  • Set-up errors were found late, by support.SSO checks the SAML file on upload and generates missing claims.
  • Secrets and access changes can lock people out.secrets are shown once, with the consequence stated, and destructive actions are typed to confirm.

DecisionsSelf-service that still works when a person helps

Give the first login a job

Why

The bank's admin should know what to do first and what is still waiting on someone else, without a call.

Trade-off

A page that will matter less once setup is done. Worth it: the first ten minutes decide whether self-service feels real.

Fix errors at upload, not in a ticket

Why

SSO shows the values the identity provider needs, then checks the SAML file as it is uploaded: each claim found or missing, and missing ones generated to copy back.

Trade-off

More logic in the product, less in support. For a self-service tier, that's the point.

Guardrails where mistakes are expensive

Why

API credentials are listed per use case, from pending to revoked. A secret is shown once, with what happens if it's lost. Disabling SSO or revoking credentials is typed to confirm.

Trade-off

Typing to confirm is slower, so it's used only where a mistake locks people or systems out.

One status vocabulary, and human help for now

Why

All three features share the same status labels and confirm pattern, so learning one teaches the others. For Q2, customer success still guides admins, so both use the same screens.

Trade-off

No guided wizard yet. It was logged for Q3 so the team could commit to a date it could meet.

Outcome

  • 7 → 1sources reconciled into one blueprintConflicts logged, open questions marked for a decision.
  • 3self-service set-up featuresSSO, IP allowlist and API client credentials.
  • ~8 hof manual set-up per customerThe back and forth self-service is designed to remove.

Reflection

What worked

Reading the company's own documents as research. AI made it fast enough to extract everything, and checking each item with its owner made the blueprint something teams trusted.

What I'd do differently

Put the set-up flows in front of bank administrators as early as possible. The people who configure SSO at a bank are rarely in the room when the contract is signed, and they use different words for the same things.
