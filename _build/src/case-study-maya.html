Maya, L5 Trip Concierge

An AI concierge for a fully self-driving car: she plans the stops, phones the shops, replans on the way, and sends the empty car to run errands on its own.

In short

The question

Once nobody has to drive, what should an in-car assistant do? Not a voice menu for the radio, but take over the errands around a trip.

What I did

A hypothesis and 16 assumptions ranked by risk, a hero scenario and conversation design, the journey and task flow, and 25 high-fidelity screens across the car and the phone.

Where it stands

Ready to test, riskiest first

No real-world results yet. The validation plan starts with the assumption everything depends on: is overheard intent welcome?

A self-driving car frees your hands, not your agenda. Navigation handles routes, not commitments: a passenger still works out the order of the stops, phones the pharmacy, checks the shop is open, and replans by hand when someone calls with a change.

Maya is for time-poor people whose evenings are a chain of pickups and errands. In the story: Michael, a father of two, with a family birthday dinner at 19:00, three people to collect and a prescription to fetch. It targets a large central touchscreen and a passive light band at the base of the windscreen, like the panoramic displays in the newest car interiors. It is a concept only, not affiliated with any car maker.

Time-poor people will hand over trip logistics to an AI that asks only what it can't know, acts on its own, follows the conversation, and makes every action visible and reversible.

The hypothesis. I'll know it's right when users arrive on time, rarely undo what Maya did, and trust her with more over time.
  • People think in needs, not addresses.the briefing is "family dinner tonight, get the kids and my wife, and that prescription". Maya asks two questions.
  • Talking costs attention in a family cabin.Maya speaks only when she needs Michael; the screen confirms, so she never reads the plan back.
  • Not every change deserves the same interruption.changes caused by the world happen silently and visibly; changes heard in conversation get a chime, the phrase heard, and Undo.
  • Trust comes from visibility, not approval steps.do first, show clearly, undo easily: limits set once, a "Done for you" ledger, and sensitive data kept off the shared screen.
  • An empty self-driving car is idle capacity.courier mode books an errand only once the shop has agreed the time, the person and the handover.

Scope stayed at one evening, one user and Level 5 only. Other errand types, guest mode, conflicts between passengers and a regulation review for empty cars went on an explicit "if I had a month" list.

Courier mode

After the family is dropped off, Maya offers to collect the suit from the dry cleaner with the empty car. She phones the shop to pay and agree the handover, and books it only then. The car checks the cabin is empty, leaves, and opens one door with a one-time code. Michael follows on his phone, view only.

What worked

Silence as a design material. Most of what Maya does is never said; deciding what not to speak, and what the screen must confirm instead, shaped every scene.

What I'd do differently

Run the riskiest test before the high-fidelity work. Everything depends on whether families welcome a car that listens, and five sessions with scripted audio would have told me early.
